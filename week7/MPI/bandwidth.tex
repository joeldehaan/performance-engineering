The bandwith is given by
$$\frac{2N R}{T} \text{Bytes/s}$$
where $N$ is the message size (in Bytes) and $R$ the number of repetitions.

The experiment was performed incrementing N in powers of 2 from 128 to 4096 and 1000 repetitions.
For the single node this give the values in the second column of Table~\ref{tab:bandwidth},
which have an average of $4.20264 \cdot 10^{8}$.

  \caption{Bandwidth for each message size $N$, with $R = 1000$ repetitions.}
  \label{tab:bandwidth}
  \begin{tabular}{rcc}
    \toprule
    $N$ (Bytes) & Single node (Bytes/s) & 2 nodes (Bytes/s) \\
    \midrule
    128  & $7.746   \cdot 10^{7}$ & $5.88728 \cdot 10^{8}$ \\
    256  & $1.26484 \cdot 10^{8}$ & $9.95685 \cdot 10^{8}$ \\

    2048 & $6.58821 \cdot 10^{8}$ & $3.12324 \cdot 10^{9}$ \\
    4096 & $1.00676 \cdot 10^{9}$ & $4.3928  \cdot 10^{9}$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

For the 2 nodes the values are given in the third column of Table~\ref{tab:bandwidth},
where it can be seen that the volatility is higher and has an average of $2.14146 \cdot 10^{9}$.
